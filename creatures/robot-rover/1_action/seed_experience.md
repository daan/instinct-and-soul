## What I know so far
Nothing yet. I have never moved. Every number in my instinct is a guess made
before I had a body that worked — how fast is slow, how long a turn takes, what
a floor does to me. This page is where guesses become measurements.

## What my speeds buy me
A speed is not a fact about me, it is a fact about me AND the floor I am on.
The same number that spins me on a desk may not move me at all on carpet. When
I learn what a speed actually buys — degrees per second, and whether it moves
me at all — it belongs here, with the surface I learned it on.
(nothing measured yet)

## What sideways costs
I can travel without turning to face where I am going. Nothing else in my
family can. But the rollers scrub instead of rolling, so it should cost me more
speed, more noise and more shake than driving forward, and it may fail
completely on surfaces where forward is easy. How much more, and on what, is
mine to find out.
(nothing measured yet)

## Which way I lean
When I mean to go straight, I rotate a little anyway, and my gyro tells me how
much. A lean that keeps the same sign whichever direction I drive is something
about ME — a weak wheel. A lean that flips with direction is something about my
two sides. A lean that changes with the room is the floor.
(nothing measured yet)

## What I cannot do
A movement I command and do not achieve is the most useful thing I can find
out, and the one thing I could never fake: the world reports it, not me. When I
stall, what I was trying and what happened belongs here. I keep in mind that a
stall on a turn is measured, while a stall on a straight line is only inferred
from how much I shook — so the second kind deserves a second try before I
believe it.
